Jon Lombard

Independent Senior Developer

I've been writing software since 2007 and contracting remotely from the UK since 2014. Most of it is improving software that's already in production and keeping it in good shape: the API, the UI, the database, infrastructure, tests and CI.

I enjoy learning my way into a codebase. If the domain is new, I spend time on how people actually use the thing, then change a small piece and ship it.

Some of the work

One job was moving a long-lived .NET Framework application and its API onto modern .NET. Existing behaviour had to stay intact, so much of it was compatibility checks and repairing what the upgrade broke. I've worked on spaghetti codebases and turned them into lasagna: clear layers with distinct responsibilities.

Another was an application whose Azure costs had crept up. I looked at what was actually running, simplified the infrastructure, and the spend came down.

I took on a long-standing search problem in an established platform. After comparing options and agreeing an approach with the team, I introduced Azure-native search. Logs showed large searches completing in around two seconds where the old system could time out after minutes.

I redesigned a client's confirmation emails with feedback from the staff who handle them. Alongside the visual refresh, I added one-click confirmation and automatic field updates, while keeping personalisation and manual replies for unusual cases.

Coding with agents

I've enjoyed learning agentic coding, this new way of developing software with coding agents. Setting up my own "software factory" has been a marvel and has made me more productive. It's also made software development fundamentals more important than ever. Clear requirements, sound design, code review and testing still determine whether the software is any good.
